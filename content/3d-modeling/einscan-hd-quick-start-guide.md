## Before 3D Head Scanning Checklist

The person being scanned should:

- remove glasses and shiny accessories
- sit comfortably and stay as still as possible
- keep a neutral facial expression
- keep lips gently closed
- look at one fixed point
- keep hair as smooth and still as practical

The scanner operator should have enough room to move completely around the person's head. Move tripping hazards out of the way and if using a tethered scanner, ensure there is enough slack in the data and power cables to move around the subject.

## 1. Start a Face / IR Scan

Use **Face Scan / IR Scan Mode** with texture enabled.

Use **Feature Alignment**.

Begin at a short working distance for good facial detail.

Keep the scanner at the correct distance:

**Blue = too far**
**Green = good**
**Red = too close**

## 2. Scan the Face First

Start in front of the face.

Move slowly and steadily.

Capture:

**face → forehead → temples → ears**

Then raise the scanner slightly to capture the face from above.

Lower and angle the scanner upward to capture:

**under the nose → chin → underside of jaw → neck**

Include part of the **shoulders**. They provide useful geometry for maintaining tracking as you move around the head.

## 3. Move Around the Head

Once the face is captured, continue around one side of the head.

Capture:

**ear → side of head → top of head → hair → back of head → opposite side → opposite ear**

Move the scanner in gentle up-and-down arcs as you travel around the person.

Do not repeatedly rescan the face. The subject's eyes and facial expression may have moved.

## 4. Check the Areas Beginners Miss

Before stopping, confirm that you captured:

**top of head**

**behind both ears**

**back of head**

**under chin**

**neck and shoulders**

## If Tracking Is Lost

**STOP moving forward.**

Do not wave the scanner around.

Return slowly to an area that was already scanned, such as:

**shoulder → ear → side of head**

Wait for tracking to recover, then continue.

## Before Finishing

Rotate the digital scan and look for obvious holes or missing areas.

If an important area is missing, return to nearby successfully scanned geometry and move gradually into the missing area.

When the head is reasonably complete, stop the scan.

## Peer Teaching - Learn it → do it → teach it

After scanning your partner:

1. Save the scan.
2. Show the next student how you operated the scanner.
3. The student you just scanned becomes the next scanner operator.
