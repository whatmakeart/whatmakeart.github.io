## Assignment Deliverables

1. Storyboard and revised production plan containing:
   - Minimum **6 storyboard frames** in sequence, representing at least 6 planned shots.
   - **One professional composition reference screenshot for every storyboard frame**, with source information.
   - Shot notes identifying framing, action, dialogue, sound, movement, timing, and virtual production requirements.
   - Your revised short script and updated virtual environment, physical set, and prop list.
   - Combine these materials into one document.
   - Label file YYYY-MM-DD Lastname Firstname VP Revised Storyboard (`.pdf`)
2. 20–60 second animatic with sound
   - Using your storyboard images, reference images, optional live-action video placeholders, or a combination.
   - Include planned cuts, shot durations, temporary dialogue when your script includes speech, and relevant sound effects or ambience.
   - Label file YYYY-MM-DD Lastname Firstname VP Animatic (`.mp4`).

Keep your Premiere project and all source images, video, and audio together in a single project folder so you can continue revising.

## Assignment Overview

Build on your shot recreation [exercises](../08-narrative-arc-and-scripting/08-03-recreate-profesional-camera-framing-and-editing-exercise-assignment.md) and original [virtual production](../../../../video/virtual-production/virtual-production.md) plan. Revise and complete your storyboard and make a pre-viz animatic, a timed edit of images, optional video placeholders, and sound that tests your sequence before recording on the XR Stage.

Add enough detail for someone else to understand how to record each shot.

### Process

#### 1. Revise Your Story and Production Plan

Update your script and production list. Confirm:

- The story has a clear beginning, middle, and end within 20 - 60 seconds.
- Every planned action has a shot, and important changes are visible or audible.
- Your plan identifies performers, assistants, props, costumes, physical set pieces, and what appears on the LED wall.

#### 2. Collect Professional Composition References

Find a professional screenshot for individual [storyboard](../../../../video/storyboards.md) frames for a minimum of 6 different screenshots showing useful [shot compositions](../../../../video/shot-types.md).

You can mix productions and media such as a wide shot from television, close-up from animation, and medium shot from a game cutscene. Films and music videos also qualify.

For each reference image, include the production title, timestamp, and link.

#### 3. Complete Your Storyboard

Storyboard every shot in viewing order, with at least 6 shots. Use drawings, staged photos, or annotated references; stick figures are fine. If a reference serves as your storyboard frame, explain what changes in your version.

Number frames, pair them with their references, and include:

- **Shot number and duration:** For example, Shot 03 — 4 seconds.
- **Framing and camera:** Shot type, height, angle, and camera movement or stationary position.
- **Action:** What performers do, where they look or move, and how they interact with objects.
- **Dialogue and sound:** Exact lines, sound effects, and ambience. Write “no dialogue” when appropriate.
- **Production needs:** What is virtual on the LED wall and what is physically on set.
- **Story purpose:** What the audience learns, notices, or feels.

Show movement with arrows or extra frames. Label frames within one shot 03A, 03B, etc.; they count as one shot.

#### 4. Build Your Animatic in Adobe Premiere

##### Optional Live-Action Video Placeholders

You may record short live-action clips with your phone or another camera and use them as placeholders for some or all shots in your animatic. **Live-action footage is optional; an animatic made entirely from still images is acceptable.**

For every placeholder clip, **frame the shot intentionally to match your selected professional reference’s framing and composition**. Consider shot size, camera height and angle, subject placement, foreground and background, and negative space. Rehearse your own planned action, movement, and dialogue within that composition. Simple locations and substitute props are fine.

Include the corresponding storyboard frame and professional reference in your document, using the same shot number as the clip. Live-action placeholders supplement your planning; the storyboard and reference requirements still apply.

##### Set Up and Import Images

1. Create a project folder with your Premiere project and subfolders for storyboard images, references, video, audio, and exports.
2. Name images in sequence: `Shot_01.png`, `Shot_02.png`, etc.
3. Create a `1920 × 1080 horizontal sequence` at 24, 30, or 60 fps.
4. Before importing stills, set `Timeline preferences > Still Image Default Duration` to about `5 seconds`. [Still image duration instructions](../../../../video/adobe-premiere-pro/change-still-image-duration-adobe-premiere.md)
5. Import images as individual stills and any optional video placeholders. [Video import instructions](../../../../video/adobe-premiere-pro/how-to-import-video-into-premiere-pro.md)

##### Edit the Sequence

1. Place images and optional video clips on the timeline in story order. Five seconds is a starting point: six 5-second shots total 30 seconds. Vary shot length to suit the story.
2. Select a still-image clip and choose `Fit to frame` in the Properties panel. [Fit to frame and other Properties controls](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/intro-to-editing/edit-video-using-the-properties-panel.html)
3. Trim or extend clips to fit the action, dialogue, and reactions. Use straight cuts unless another transition serves the story.

##### Suggest Camera Movement When Useful

To suggest a pan, tilt, zoom, or push-in with a still image:

1. Select the clip and open `Window > Effect Controls > Motion`.
2. At the clip’s beginning, set the starting composition. Click the stopwatch beside `Position`, `Scale`, or both to create keyframes. [Motion controls for Position and Scale](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/commonly-used-effects/apply-motion-effect.html)
3. Near the clip’s end, change those values to create ending keyframes.
4. Play back and adjust the movement and timing. [Keyframe instructions](../../../../video/adobe-premiere-pro/add-keyframes-adobe-premiere.md)

Use Position for pans/tilts and Scale for zooms/push-ins. Avoid exposing image edges. These moves suggest camera movement without changing 3D perspective. Keep stationary shots still.

##### Add Sound and Revise

- Record all scripted dialogue at a believable pace using a phone or microphone.
- Place important sound effects and ambience where they occur. Stories without dialogue still need sound.
- Use music only when it supports the scene; keep dialogue and important sounds clear.
- Watch the full edit without stopping. Fix rushed, confusing, repetitive, or slow sections.

Rough acting, animation, and sound are fine if the experience and timing are clear.

#### 5. Export and Check Your Submission

[Export](../../../../video/adobe-premiere-pro/export-video-adobe-premiere.md) the animatic as an H.264 `MP4` with audio, 1920x1080, 24/30/60 fps. Watch the exported file before submitting. [How to Export Video from Adobe Premiere](../../../../video/adobe-premiere-pro/export-video-adobe-premiere.md)
