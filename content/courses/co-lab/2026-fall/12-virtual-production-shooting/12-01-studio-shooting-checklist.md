## Before Recording

1. Choose the most important shots you can complete today. Use your existing script and storyboard; revise the plan if a simpler setup works better.
2. Agree on who will perform, handle props, assist the camera or lights, and note useful takes. Roles can change between setups.
3. Finish the parts of the prop or environment the camera will see. Test the framing before adding detail elsewhere.
4. With the instructor, check the camera image, lighting, virtual background, and any sound you need to record.

## Record and Review

1. Rehearse the action once. Check where the performer moves and looks.
2. Identify the shot and take out loud or in a simple written note.
3. Start recording, allow a short pause, perform the action, pause again, then stop. A couple of extra seconds at either end gives you room to trim.
4. Play back the recording. Is the action visible? Is the subject in focus? Is needed sound understandable? Does the background look right through the camera?
5. Repeat when a problem interferes with the idea. Capture a closer view, reaction, or detail when it will help the edit. You do not need every possible camera angle.

For matching shots, check prop placement, performer movement, and lighting. A quick setup photo and a note about the best take can help you repeat the scene.

## Before Leaving

- Confirm where the recordings are stored and that every collaborator can access the shared media.
- Copy the complete recording folders and keep a backup separate from your working copy. Preserve the original files and folder names.
- Write down any missing shot, sound, prop, or environment change.

Use this as a studio checklist. There is no separate submission or grade for this page.
