## Before Recording

1. Choose the most important shots you can complete today. Review your existing script and storyboard. Revise the plan if a simpler setup works better.
2. Agree on who will perform, handle props, assist the camera or lights, and note useful takes. Roles can change between setups.
3. Finish the parts of the prop or environment the camera will see. Test the framing before adding detail elsewhere.
4. Check the camera image, lighting, virtual background, and any sound you need to record.

## Record and Review

1. Rehearse the action once before recording. Check where each performer moves and looks.
2. Start recording.
   - Identify the shot and take out loud so it baked in the recording or in a simple written note.
   - Allow a short pause, perform the action, pause again, then stop. A couple of extra seconds at either end gives you room to trim.
3. Play back the recording. Is the action visible? Is the subject in focus? Does the background look right through the camera? Avoid focal lengths that show the individual LEDs.
4. Repeat the shot once while everyone is in place.
5. Before moving to a new setup capture more coverage of the shot.
   - Capture a closer view, reaction, or detail when it will help the edit. _(Remember: Change angle and distance.)_
   - You do not need every possible camera angle.
6. For matching shots, check prop placement, performer movement, and lighting. A quick setup photo and a note about the best take can help you repeat the scene.

## Before Leaving

- Confirm where the recordings are stored. Label the files consistently. Make sure every collaborator can access the shared media.
- Copy the complete recording folders and keep a backup separate from your working copy. Preserve the original files and folder names.
- Write down a list of missing shots, sounds, props, or environment changes.
