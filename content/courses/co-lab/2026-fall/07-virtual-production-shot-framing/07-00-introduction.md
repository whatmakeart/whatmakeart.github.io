## Overview

In the XR Studio, students use screenshots from films and other moving image media as visual references for composing virtual production shots. A shot is one continuous camera view. A cut is the transition from one shot to another. A scene may contain many different shots and cuts.

Students examine how shot size, camera angle, subject placement, and foreground-to-background relationships direct the viewer's attention. They use rule-of-thirds overlays to analyze existing compositions without altering the original framing. In addition, learners track the duration and purpose of consecutive shots to connect composition directly with editing rhythm. These deliberate observations help them plan a cohesive sequence that they can reproduce using a performer, a physical camera, and a virtual environment.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish a shot, a cut, and a scene
- Identify common shot sizes, camera angles, and camera movements
- Analyze subject placement, negative space, and depth within a frame
- Add a visible rule-of-thirds overlay while preserving the original composition
- Measure shot durations and describe how the timing of cuts affects a sequence
- Explain how camera and performer placement could reproduce a reference composition in the XR Studio
