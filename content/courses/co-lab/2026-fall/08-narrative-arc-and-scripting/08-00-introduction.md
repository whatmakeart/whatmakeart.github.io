## Overview

Students present their shot framing screenshots and discuss the limits of using composition frameworks such as the rule of thirds and why it might be best to [forget the Rule of Thirds](../../../../articles/forget-the-rule-of-thirds.md).

Students take screen recording captures of the visual sequences that they analyzed for homework. Using [Adobe Premiere](../../../../video/adobe-premiere-pro/adobe-premiere.md)'s automated scene and shot detection tools, they break up the sequents into the original shots on the timeline. Students then use these shots and timings as a guide to recrate shot for shot recordings of the sequence.

Students turn their observations about framing and editing into a short virtual production idea. Use a [narrative arc](../../../../video/narrative-arc.md) or [story spine](../../../../video/story-spine.md) to identify what changes over time. The project may tell a conventional story, stage an impossible event, or develop an abstract visual sequence, but its progression should be intentional. Translate the idea into a brief script or action outline, [storyboards](../../../../video/storyboards.md), and a shot list. Plan the camera position, performer or object placement, foreground and background, sound, and physical space required for each shot. Agree on responsibilities and choose a scope your team can build, test, shoot, and edit with the available studio time.

## Learning Objectives

- Describe a sequence's beginning, development, and ending
- Write an action outline that identifies visible events and important sounds
- Draw storyboard panels that communicate framing, action, and camera movement
- Convert a storyboard into a shot list with a clear purpose for each shot
- Identify the physical props, costumes, digital assets, and audio needed for production
- Evaluate whether planned shots fit the available stage, time, and resources
- Assign team responsibilities and communicate the next production steps
