## Overview

Students continue production to capture all essential material, recording their individual project sequences using the settings, technical workflows, and timing established during rehearsal, storyboarding, and animatics. Working collaboratively within assigned production groups, students rotate through crew responsibilities to ensure each member gathers necessary views, performances, reactions, prop handling, and audio—adapting setups flexibly when recorded footage suggests stronger visual solutions. Group members review takes together while the studio setup remains active so project owners can log results and address technical or performance issues immediately.

Following production, each student organizes, backs up, and preserves original media before assembling an individual rough cut. Students analyze this early cut during class alongside collaborators, examining shot selection, rhythm, screen direction, missing coverage, continuity errors, and sound gaps. Because each student maintains independent editorial control over shot progression and pacing—even when drawing from shared footage—peer feedback helps creators pinpoint structural flaws and compile a prioritized pickup and reshoot list. Before Thanksgiving break, students submit their third and final progress report and outline a focused shooting plan for the fourth XR Studio session on December 2. **There is no class on November 25 and no homework assigned during Thanksgiving break**, requiring all preparatory work and reports to be completed prior to the recess.

## Learning Objectives

- Coordinate studio production using an organized shot list, animatics, and assigned crew roles.
- Capture essential coverage, performances, and audio with adequate edit handles before the final stage session.
- Maintain continuity of movement, props, eyelines, and screen direction across setups.
- Log takes accurately, review playback on set, and identify viable performances for the edit.
- Secure original footage through redundant media backups before post-production begins.
- Assemble and evaluate an individual rough cut to assess story progression, rhythm, and clarity.
- Identify narrative, continuity, timing, and sound gaps to compile a focused, prioritized pickup and reshoot list.
- Exchange targeted peer feedback to refine personal edits and finalize the shoot plan ahead of the holiday break.

## Key Terms

- **Continuity:** Consistency of action, appearance, props, and spatial relationships across multiple shots and setups.
- **Coverage:** The range of camera setups, angles, and shot sizes captured to give the editor flexible storytelling choices.
- **Edit Handle:** Extra recorded footage before an action starts and after it concludes to facilitate clean transitions.
- **Media Backup:** The practice of copying and preserving camera card files across multiple secure storage drives before editing.
- **Pacing:** The rhythm and temporal flow established through shot lengths, actor movement, and audio cutting.
- **Picture Edit:** The active phase of choosing, sequencing, and trimming visual elements to craft a coherent narrative.
- **Pickup Shot:** An added shot captured to fill an editorial gap, clarify an action, or bridge a transition in an existing sequence.
- **Reshoot:** A newly recorded version of an entire scene or angle intended to replace a previously recorded take.
- **Room Tone:** The ambient background noise of the stage or filming environment recorded in silence for sound continuity.
- **Rough Cut:** An early assembly of picture and sound used to test pacing, continuity, narrative structure, and coverage needs.
- **Shot Log:** A written or digital on-set tracking record noting take numbers, camera settings, scene details, and directors' preferred takes.
- **Take:** Each individual recorded attempt of a specific shot or action from slate to cut.
