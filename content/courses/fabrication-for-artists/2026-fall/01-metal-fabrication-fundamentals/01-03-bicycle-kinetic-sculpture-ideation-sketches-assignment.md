## Assignment Deliverables

## 30–45 Minute Ideation Sketching

Before cutting the bicycle apart, spend **30–45 minutes generating ideas through drawing**.

Do not spend the entire time making one polished drawing. Make many quick sketches and allow one idea to lead to another.

Begin by drawing at least **10 quick possibilities**. Then select several promising ideas and make more developed drawings showing how the sculpture might actually move.

Explore:

- What rotates?
- What stays stationary?
- What does the viewer touch?
- Where does the movement begin?
- How is movement transferred somewhere else?
- Could one wheel turn several other wheels?
- Could a wheel drive another wheel through friction?
- Could a belt connect two distant rotating parts?
- Could the headset become a pivot?
- Could the bottom bracket rotate something other than a bicycle wheel?
- What happens if bicycle components are duplicated or moved to unexpected locations?
- What happens if the sculpture is extremely tall, long, wide, unstable-looking, awkward, humorous, repetitive, or absurd?

Your sketches **do not need to solve every fabrication problem**. At this stage, prioritize possibilities. We will use fabrication, testing, and prototyping to determine what actually works.

## Ideation Sketch Documentation

Document your sketches. Sketches may be scanned or photographed, but the final documentation should clearly show the drawings rather than the environment around them.

When photographing sketches:

- Place the paper on a flat surface.
- Use bright, even lighting.
- Avoid shadows across the page.
- Hold the camera directly above and parallel to the paper.
- Fill the image frame with the page.
- Keep the entire drawing in focus.
- Crop out unnecessary background.
- Correct rotation and perspective if necessary.
- Adjust exposure so the paper appears light and the drawing remains clearly visible.

A phone camera is sufficient when used carefully. Combine multiple pages into a clearly organized PDF or submit clean individual images.

## Artist + Project References

These references approach bicycles in very different ways. Some retain the bicycle as a human-powered machine, while others use bicycle wheels, bearings, chains, frames, or other components as raw material for kinetic sculpture.

1. **Koichiro Azuma** — especially relevant for large interactive sculptures constructed from discarded bicycles and fabricated steel. [Koichiro Azuma — Works](https://koichiro-azuma.com/en/home/)
2. **Jean Tinguely** — kinetic machines built from wheels, belts, motors, scrap metal, and found mechanisms. [Museum Tinguely — Jean Tinguely](https://www.tinguely.ch/en/tinguely-collection-conservation/tinguely-biographie.html)
3. **Marcel Duchamp** — _Bicycle Wheel_ demonstrates the bicycle wheel as both found object and freely rotating sculpture. [MoMA — Bicycle Wheel](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81631?art_term_slug=readymade&sov_referrer=art_term&utm_source=chatgpt.com)
4. **Ai Weiwei** — uses repeated bicycle forms to transform a familiar functional object into large-scale sculpture and installation. [Hirshhorn — Forever Bicycles](https://hirshhorn.si.edu/explore/ai-weiwei-forever-bicycles/)
5. **Diane Landry** — motorized installations incorporating bicycle wheels, pulleys, timing belts, bearings, light, and other materials. [Diane Landry — Knight of Infinite Resignation](https://dianelandry.com/installations-en/knight-of-infinite-resignation-2/)
6. **Nemo Gould** — kinetic found-object machines useful for studying mechanisms, cranks, linkages, and imaginative reuse. [Nemo Gould — Kinetic Sculpture](https://nemogould.com/)
7. **Mark Grieve** — co-creator of _Cyclisk_, a monumental sculpture made from hundreds of discarded bicycles. [Cyclisk Public Art Project](https://www.srcity.org/773/Cyclisk-Public-Art-Project)
8. **Ilana Spector** — co-creator and fabricator of _Cyclisk_. [Cyclisk Public Art Project](https://www.srcity.org/773/Cyclisk-Public-Art-Project)
9. **Cyclecide** — artist collective transforming junk bicycles into tall bikes, unusual vehicles, kinetic sculptures, and pedal-powered carnival rides. [Cyclecide Bike Rodeo](https://www.cyclecide.com/beer/press-release/)
10. **Chris Cole** — kinetic sculptor who transforms salvaged bicycle and mechanical parts into moving creatures and machines. [Chris Cole Designs](https://www.chriscoledesigns.com/bend-magazine-feature)
11. **Juan Martinez** — articulated, rideable animal sculptures fabricated from bicycle components and metal. [Juan Martinez — Sculpture Magazine](https://sculpturemagazine.art/life-and-spirit-a-conversation-with-juan-martinez/)
12. **Melissa Daubert** — interactive kinetic sculpture incorporating bicycle parts, cranks, wheels, and audience activation. [Melissa Daubert](https://melissa-daubert.com/)
13. **Alex Rodriguez / Dr. ARod** — kinetic sculptures using bicycle wheels and components as rotating systems. [Dr. ARod's Art](https://drarods.art/portfolio)
14. **Patricia Vader** — kinetic and public sculptures incorporating bicycle wheels and hubs. [Patricia Vader — RA](https://cityartmankato.com/cityart/ra/)
15. **Jake Beckman** — _RotaFlora_, a 34-foot sculpture fabricated from bicycle wheels. [RotaFlora — Ohio Outdoor Sculpture](https://sculpturecenter.org/oosi/items/show/1631)
16. **Cindy Chinn** — sculptures made from recycled bicycle tires, wheels, pedals, and other components, including kinetic works activated by pedals. [Cindy Chinn — Re-Tired Artwork](https://cindychinn.com/re-tired-artwork-from-recycled-bicycle-tires-cindy-chinn/)
17. **Rachel Dorn** — combines discarded bicycle components with ceramic and mixed-media sculpture. [Rachel Dorn — Bicycle Parts Sculpture](https://www.racheldorn.com/sram)
18. **Keith Bryant** — combines bicycle components, sprockets, wood, glass, and found objects into mixed-media sculpture. [Keith Bryant — Machinations of Sprockets and Wood](https://www.knoxalliance.com/keith-bryant-the-machinations-of-sprockets-and-wood/)
19. **Daria Wendland** — large installations constructed from salvaged bicycle wheels. [Daria Wendland — Abgefahren](https://www.art-visuals.de/wendland/installation.html)
20. **Norman Tuck** — interactive kinetic sculpture using bicycle wheels, motors, chains, and deceptively simple mechanical systems. [Norman Tuck — Lariat Chain](https://www.normantuck.com/lariatchain)

When looking at these references, do not concentrate only on what the finished sculpture looks like. Look at **how motion is produced and transferred**: wheels, axles, bearings, belts, chains, pulleys, cranks, friction, gravity, motors, and the body can all become sculptural materials.
