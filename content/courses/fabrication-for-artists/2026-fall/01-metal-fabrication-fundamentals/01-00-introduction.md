## Overview

This first module introduces the metal shop and the fundamental tools, materials, and processes used for [metal fabrication](../../../../metalworking/metal-fabrication.md). We will begin with basic metal cutting, surface preparation, and safe use of common shop equipment before moving into [welding](../../../../metalworking/welding.md) and tubular fabrication in the following weeks.

The bicycle will serve as the starting structure for exploring these processes. A bicycle frame is a useful fabrication exercise because it combines thin wall steel tubing, welded joints, mechanical components, and a structure designed to respond to forces generated by a human body. Students will select an upcycled bicycle frame from the Ohio City Bike Co-op and investigate how it can be cut apart, rearranged, extended, duplicated, or combined with other bicycle components to create a new kinetic sculpture. The finished work might remain rideable and pedal-powered, or the bicycle components may become part of a completely different mechanical or sculptural form.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify common metal shop tools and describe their basic functions.
- Follow appropriate safety procedures when working with metal-cutting and surface-preparation equipment.
- Identify major bicycle frame tubes and mechanical components.
- Recognize how an existing manufactured object can be treated as raw material for sculpture.
- Safely cut and disassemble portions of a steel bicycle frame.
- Prepare painted or coated steel surfaces for welding and fabrication.
- Generate multiple sketches that explore structural and kinetic transformations of an existing bicycle.
- Consider how structure, movement, material, and evidence of fabrication can contribute to the meaning and experience of an artwork.

## Key Terms

- Stock: Material supplied in a standard shape or size before fabrication.
- Layout: Transferring measurements and cut locations onto material.
- Kerf: The width of material removed by a cutting tool.
- Burr: A sharp raised edge left after cutting or drilling.
- Surface preparation: Cleaning and preparing a surface for a later operation.
- Kinetic sculpture: An artwork in which actual movement is part of the work.
