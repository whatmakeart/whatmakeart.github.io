## Measure the Material You Have

Nominal material names are not reliable finished dimensions. Measure stock thickness, width, and usable length before designing a joint or laying out a group of parts. Use one unit system consistently on each drawing.

Make a small drawing with overall dimensions, part thicknesses, connection locations, and the object or space the structure will serve. Mark which dimensions matter most for fit.

## Cut List

<div class="responsive-table-markdown">

| Part                | Material / actual thickness      | Quantity    | Finished length × width             | Notes                     |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Base                | Student-selected wood or plywood | 1           | Enter measured design dimensions    | Mark reference face       |
| Upright or side     | Same or compatible stock         | As designed | Enter dimensions                    | Check joint thickness     |
| Support / stretcher | As designed                      | As designed | Enter dimensions                    | Leave tool access         |
| Test pieces         | Offcuts of the same stock        | As needed   | Large enough for the approved setup | Test fasteners and finish |

</div>
Account for the kerf between parts. Keep rough-cut allowances separate from finished dimensions. For repeated parts, use an approved stop or template after confirming the first part; do not rely on copying each part from the last.

## Plan Connections Now

Sketch how the steel brackets and plastic element will attach. Show fastener access, clearance for removal, and the order of assembly. A full-size cardboard model can reveal a problem before a tool is used.
